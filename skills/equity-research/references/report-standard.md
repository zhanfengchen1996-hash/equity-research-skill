# English Report Standard

Write a finished investment report for a reader evaluating one company. Use the outline below to organize the reasoning and avoid repeated explanations. The opening summary synthesizes the complete report after the body has been drafted.

## Chapter ownership

| Section | Analytical responsibility | Useful exhibit |
|---|---|---|
| Executive Summary | State the investment judgment, decisive evidence and separate short/long horizons | Compact research metadata |
| 1 Business and Products | Explain products, buyers, uses, charging models, revenue categories and delivery | Product table, revenue mix, value chain |
| 2 Industry and Market Outlook | Define classification and the actual market; explain supply, demand, pricing and the cycle | Dated industry measures |
| 3 Competition and Competitive Advantage | Integrate all five competitive forces and the firm's strategy | Like-for-like peer comparison |
| 4 Financial Performance and Cash Flow | Attribute growth; explain earnings quality, expectations, cash generation and funding | Earnings comparison and cash/funding bridge |
| 5 Next Quarter Revenue Forecast | Explain both forecast paths, their reconciliation and the guidance comparison | Driver tables and low/base/high ranges |
| 6 Economic and Policy Environment | Explain incremental PESTLE effects on this company's economics | A causal diagram where it clarifies the mechanism |
| 7 Valuation and Investment Bank Views | Compare bank assumptions and develop an independent valuation | Dated bank views and valuation scenarios |
| 8 Share Price Trends and Near Term View | Interpret US price structure, relative performance, liquidity and volatility | Price/benchmark chart and selected metrics |
| 9 Long Term Outlook and Investment Conclusion | Synthesize competitive durability, cash returns, capital allocation and downside | A concise final judgment |
| Appendix | Preserve sources, chapter mappings and material calculation conventions | Evidence register and methods |

Define a product in chapter 1, its market in chapter 2 and its competitive advantage in chapter 3. Explain the full current funding position in chapter 4; chapter 6 adds the effect of external changes. Derive the quarterly forecast in chapter 5; chapter 7 explains its valuation significance. A fact may recur only when it supports a distinct inference.

## Depth and readability

A full report normally has 4,000–6,000 narrative words plus tables and the appendix. The executive summary normally has 200–300 words. Respect a different length requested by the user and allocate more space to the factors that determine value.

Use two or three substantive subsections in most chapters. The forecast chapter has four: integrated outlook, top-down drivers, bottom-up drivers, and guidance/reconciliation. Use connected paragraphs to explain each argument. Charts support the reasoning; they do not replace it. Avoid equal-length framework summaries when one factor is substantially more important than the others.

Use plain English headings that name the topic. Explain specialist terms on first use and describe technical product features through customer value, revenue, cost or capital intensity. Keep dates and fiscal periods explicit. Use readable units such as USD million or USD billion consistently, retaining precise inputs in calculation tables.

## Direct investment language

State the judgment, identify its basis and explain how the business mechanism affects earnings, cash or valuation. Use explicit estimates and assumptions where the analysis requires forecasting. A clear conclusion can be positive, negative or neutral when the evidence and weighting justify it.

Remove vague attitudes such as “worth monitoring,” “cautiously optimistic,” “remains reasonable,” “deserves consideration” and “it remains to be seen.” Replace them with the financial implication that the reader can assess. Avoid slogans, forced contrasts, repeated “not X but Y” constructions and generic praise of management or a technology.

Do not add monitoring lists, trigger-condition sections or verification agendas. Discuss material risks as economic exposures: the amount or timing at issue, the mechanism and the effect on the conclusion. Do not append repetitive self-protective disclaimers. Any disclosure explicitly required for the user's publishing context belongs in one appropriate location and must remain accurate.

Preserve uncertainty with specific wording: an estimate is an estimate, a plan is a plan, and a contingent economic relationship keeps its conditions. Clear writing does not transform modeled outcomes into reported facts or erase an adverse result.

### Illustrative wording

The following figures are hypothetical and demonstrate prose only.

> We estimate next-quarter revenue of USD 540 million, 8% above management's USD 500 million midpoint. The difference comes from one additional month of billable capacity at the new facility; the model holds price per unit constant. That timing assumption adds USD 40 million to revenue and explains the gap to guidance.

> A 100 bp increase in funding cost adds USD 5 million of annual interest on USD 500 million of new debt. For a full quarter outstanding, the additional interest is USD 1.25 million before capitalization and tax effects. Construction spending therefore raises the financing burden before the project contributes a full quarter of revenue.

These are numerical explanations, not estimates for a named company. Use company-specific evidence and computed results in an actual report.

## Sources and exhibits

Keep the prose free of repetitive inline citation markers and links. Maintain claim-to-source mappings in research notes and publish a consolidated appendix listing source ID, title, publisher, publication date, URL and relevant sections. Identify media summaries of bank research as such.

Every quantitative exhibit identifies its reporting period, units, currency, scope and whether values are actuals, guidance or estimates. A caption explains the main comparison. Visual width and area must correspond to the stated values. Use a revenue-share chart only when the categories reconcile to a meaningful common denominator.

Use one report body for all requested formats. Check table widths, figure labels, page breaks, working links and the absence of placeholders after export. Preserve numbers and source mappings when changing format or simplifying prose.
